## FEAT-lyrics-viewer-playback — transport + queue inside the lyrics viewer (2026-08-01 → 2026-08-03)

The full-screen live lyrics viewer stopped being read-only. Three steps, all shipped and prod-smoked, all confirmed by the owner on a real device, plus one open question closed two days later.

- **Step 1 — bottom transport bar** (front #327 `9d89837` / deploy 30687122041, prod smoke 19/0). ⏮ ⏸/▶ ⏭ + an inert ☰ 대기열, always visible on the live entry. The plan assumed `sendPlayerCommand` already dispatched `MYBLOG_PLAYBACK_CHANGED`; a browser check disproved it (the RFC's cited line was inside `sendConnectPlay`, a different function; a live listener saw 0 events from ⏯⏭⏮). Step 1 therefore owned both the optimistic `playing` write — the only mechanism available, since the resync path is behind a 1.5 s floor — and the post-skip identity read. Pause freezes the anchor at the current estimate so resume starts from truth. Two incidental fixes: `.lyv-return` (the browse countdown ring) was occupying the bar's footprint, and `measureRef` cached `boxH` without invalidating on `notice`.
- **Step 2 — queue screen** (front #328 `d66f173` / deploy 30688315730, prod smoke 19/0). A full-panel swap, not an overlaid sheet: the viewer's vertical drag is already line-stepping, so a sheet would have forced gesture arbitration at a boundary. Returning from the queue **lands** rather than slides — `positionedRef` is reset while the queue is up, so the viewer stands on the *current* line on return, and the whole round trip costs zero `/api/lyrics` calls. 204 is an empty queue, not a failure.
- **Step 3 — queue-row jump** (front #329 `2a4fd9e` / deploy 30708914030, follow-up #330 `aefcb02` / deploy 30709949344, prod smoke 19/0 each). Try `context_uri` + `offset` → fall back to `uris: [tapped, ...visible tail]` → in-panel notice. The fallback carries the **tail**, not the lone track: `uris` replaces the context, so a single-track fallback would silently delete everything queued behind the tap. The chain lives in `queueJump.ts` so "the tail is carried" is pinned by a unit test rather than a clickthrough.
- **OQ1** — closed 2026-08-01 by minting the real grant, not by reading the constant that requests it. The queue endpoint answered **200** (19 items, and 200 even while paused); `STREAMING_SCOPES` under-describes what was actually consented. Two lessons carried forward: a requested-scope constant is not evidence of a granted scope, and the question was never owner-only — the browser's token is minted server-side from an SSM secret, so it was answerable without a human. It sat blocked on the owner for a day for nothing.
- **OQ2** — closed 2026-08-01 by the owner: try → fall back → give up (above). Side effect on the design: rows became tappable by default, because the fallback removes the need to predict which rows are reachable.
- **OQ4 — closed 2026-08-03** (front #342 `a53d0a4` / deploy 30772039596, prod smoke 19/0). Should `sendPlayerCommand` dispatch `MYBLOG_PLAYBACK_CHANGED`? Owner: yes. Two premises the RFC carried into that question were wrong:
  - "the log site has to move too" over-read its own finding. A residual is predicted-minus-actual **position**, and a track change destroys the prediction, so ⏭/⏮ have nothing to measure — not a lost measurement. The empty half was ⏸/▶: same track, measurable, and no read happened after them at all.
  - the dispatch was not one line. `NowPlaying.applyLive` folded `paused` into `idle` (clearing `moment`, resetting `paused`), so the first ⏸ arriving from another surface would have wiped the progress bar, duration, device hint and mode controls and flipped the button back to ▶ — the card collapsing in response to its own pause. `playPause`'s missing confirmation read was the workaround that kept this invisible; only a deliverable paused read could expose it, and until this change nothing could deliver one.

  Shipped with three guards: `applyLive` renders `paused` alongside `playing`; the NowPlaying listener ignores events raised by its own commands (`controlBusyRef`); the viewer gains `awaitingPlayState`, the third member of the `awaitingTrack`/`awaitingChangeFrom` family. Modes (shuffle/repeat/volume) deliberately do not dispatch — they move neither playhead nor track.

  `logResidual` also gained `readState`. `estimateMs` ages an anchor by wall time with no notion of pause, so a sample taken against a held player carries the elapsed round-trip inside it: the two live samples were **37,084 ms** (`readState: 'paused'`) and **89 ms** (`readState: 'playing'`), side by side in one series and indistinguishable without the label. The dispatch alone would have opened a channel that arrives unreadable.

**Owner real-device passes.** Steps 1+2 on 2026-08-01 (queue scrolls past the fold, `←` returns to the correct lyric line, ⏭/⏸ work). One false alarm resolved on the way — a queue that looked wrong was **repeat mode**, not a bug; repeated ids are why rows are keyed `${id}:${index}`. Step 3 on 2026-08-02: after tapping a manually-queued song on the phone, the songs behind it were still in the Spotify app's queue — the one claim that only a real device could settle.

**Two verification lessons this RFC paid for, both now standing rules.**

1. **Model the lag or the harness flatters the code.** `PUT /me/player/play` is applied asynchronously — the 204 is an acknowledgement, and `GET /me/player` right after it can still report the previous track. The Step 3 stub changed its state in the same tick as the 204, which made that race unrepresentable, and a "one read after the command" bug passed verification and shipped. The owner found it the same day. Fixed in front #333 by extracting `confirmTransport()` and putting **both** post-command readers on it. The generalisable rule is not "model the lag" — that was already written down — it is **when a race is found, grep the file for every other site of the same shape before closing it**. OQ4 became the third instance of that same shape.
2. **Proving a change reached prod when it adds no new string.** The `/members/` island crawl must run to a **fixed point, not a fixed depth** — `LyricsViewer` sits three levels down, and a two-level crawl silently omitted it, making a shipped fix read as missing. Identifiers are minified, so grepping for a new function name always fails; numeric constants and **object keys** survive. OQ4's verification used `readState:` as the marker and crawled 46 chunks.

PRs: front #327 / #328 / #329 / #330 / #333 / #342; ws #761 / #763 / #764 / #769 / #770 / #771 / #772 / #779 / #785 and this close-out. RFC archived → `docs/archive/done/rfcs/FEAT-lyrics-viewer-playback.md`.

## BUG-20 — LikedBoard's bucket-picker skipped the spotify_library exclusion guard (2026-08-05)

Found by an independent architecture audit of entity-interaction fragmentation across `myblog_front`
(scope: domains `ARCH-entity-interaction-v2` explicitly excludes — reviews/memos, playback, lyrics
host, bucket-add flows — not a step of that RFC). Not a design question; two plain bugs, fixed same
session as ad hoc fixes (front #355, `734925e`).

- **The bug**: `LikedBoard.tsx`'s "My Buckit에 담기" promote flow passed an unfiltered bucket tree to
  `BucketPickerSheet` (no `skip`), so a member could manually add a liked track's album into the
  sync-owned `kind='spotify_library'` mirror bucket. Three other call sites
  (`BucketBoard.tsx`, `AddToBucketMenu.tsx`, `pocketBuckit/leaf.ts`) already enforced the exclusion —
  two by hardcoding the string literal, one via the `SLIB_KIND` constant — so the rule had no single
  owner and a fourth surface simply omitted it. The backend does not backstop this either
  (`_assert_item_type_allowed` gates only on `bucket.type`, never `kind`).
- **The fix**: one exported predicate, `isManualAddTarget` (`lib/buckets.ts`), and all four call sites
  now route through it instead of re-testing `kind` independently.
- **A second, unrelated bug found in the same audit and fixed in the same PR**: `BucketBoard.tsx`'s
  `trashAlbum` could forward a still-optimistic `temp:${Date.now()}:${albumId}` id (from `copyAlbum`'s
  pre-resolution tile) straight to `DELETE /api/buckets/{id}/items/{itemId}` if the tile was trashed
  before its `addBucketItem` POST resolved. Fixed by marking the temp id instead; the POST's own
  resolution handler now deletes the real row if/when it lands, rather than sending a garbage id or
  silently resurrecting the item after the user asked to trash it.
- **Verification**: `pnpm lint` clean, `pnpm exec astro check` 0 errors, `pnpm test` 463/463
  (+7 new — `lib/buckets.test.ts` for `isManualAddTarget`, `BucketPickerSheet.test.tsx` for the `skip`
  contract; that component had zero test coverage before this PR). Deploy's own post-deploy health
  smoke passed; general prod smoke (`scripts/smoke.sh prod`) 19/19. **Not independently reproduced live
  via CDP** — the smoke account has never synced Spotify, so exercising the exact `spotify_library`
  scenario would need the same account-bucket-relabeling technique `ARCH-entity-interaction-v2` Step 3
  used, judged disproportionate effort for a defensive guard fix with deterministic component-test
  coverage of the exact contract fixed.

Full audit findings (root cause, target architecture, 8 guardrails, priority migration plan for the
remaining items) recorded in memory `project-arch-entity-interaction-v2-domain-audit`; the broader
migration is tracked as `ARCH-entity-interaction-domain-audit` (see `docs/plan.md`).

## FIX — AlbumCard/TrackRow/BucketBoard tiles had zero drag visual feedback (2026-08-10)

`AlbumCard` and `TrackRow` (the canonical drag sources per `ARCH-entity-interaction-v2` /
`ARCH-album-card-contract-and-composition`) had no drag CSS at all: no cursor hint, no source-card
state while a drag is in flight. `BucketBoard`'s own tile wrapper already toggled an `.lf-is-dragging`
class on dragstart/dragend, but no rule ever targeted it, so a dragged bucket tile gave no feedback
either — the class existed unstyled since the tile adapter was written.

- `AlbumCard`: new `data-dragging` state (set in the existing `onDragStart`/`onDragEnd`) drives
  `cursor: grab`/`grabbing` on the open-hit button + opacity 0.45 while dragging (`album-card.css`).
- `TrackRow`: same states via the component's existing inline-style convention.
- `BucketBoard` tiles: `.lf-drag-handle` gets `cursor: grab` on fine pointers (`layout.css`);
  `.lf-is-dragging` gets the opacity dim (`widgets.css`).

All three mirror the opacity-only D-series invariant already used by `crate-trash-card`/Pocket-tray
drop targets — no drag source ever reflows.

Found a genuinely dead branch in the same audit: `fix/arch-album-card-drag-feedback` (local, no PR)
pointed at a commit already 8 commits behind `main` with zero unique work on it — its worktree had
already been reaped by the OS temp cleaner. Deleted; not a loss, nothing was ever built on it.

**Verification**: `pnpm lint` / `pnpm exec astro check` (0 errors) / `pnpm test` (583 passed, incl. 2
new drag-state assertions) all green. Real-Chrome verification used a static fixture loading the
actual `album-card.css`/`layout.css`/`widgets.css` files in their real `@import` order (not the live
authed app — that needs a running backend + Cognito session) and read computed `cursor`/`opacity` —
this caught and fixed two real bugs before they shipped: a cascade-order tie (`.bb-tile{cursor:pointer}`
vs `.lf-drag-handle{cursor:grab}`, equal specificity, order-dependent) and a `:active`-only grabbing
rule that would not have survived an actual native HTML5 drag. Deployed (front run 31345899304), prod
smoke 19/19 (`scripts/smoke.sh prod`).

PR: front #393 (`fa1f664`).

## D-2 (2026-07-26 audit) closed with no action — 2026-08-16

Owner decision: close the last open row of the 2026-07-26 system audit without shipping a fix. The
section header moved from "남은 것 = D-2 owner 결정뿐" to "전부 종료" as a result; nothing from that audit
remains open.

**The defect is real.** `cli_engine.py:131-146` retries only `LLMValidationError`, so an
`LLMTransientError` propagates out of the engine on the first occurrence instead of being retried there.

**No caller is exposed to it.** The 2026-08-09 re-measurement walked every caller: `buckit_nightly.py`
wraps its calls in its own 3×60s retry (added 2026-07-31); `research_poller.py`,
`lyrics_translate_poller.py`, and `genius_translate_poller.py` are self-healing — a failed item is simply
not claimed and the next tick picks it up; `editor_buckit.py` is manual single-fire by design, where a
visible failure is the intended behaviour. Exposed paths: **zero**.

**Why not fix it anyway.** The engine lives in `myblog_shared_db`, so the fix is a cross-repo change plus a
re-pin in backend, music, and worker — three deploys and three verification gates to harden a path that no
caller currently reaches. That is defence-in-depth, and `feedback-necessity-gate-reviews` says a
speculative harden with no concrete harm does not earn that cost. Recorded here rather than dropped
silently, because the point of this entry is the *reasoning for not fixing it* — if a future caller is
added without its own retry, reopen with a fresh `plan.md` row and cite this decision.
