# A2 anchors — GPT-6 Astra

All albums, artists, sources, quotations and task records below are **fictional**. The recurring example concerns Mara Vale’s *Glass Orchard*, whose assigned target is the standard digital edition. Source and audit descriptions accompanying excerpts are part of each example’s observable evidence.

These are dimension-specific anchors, not whole-note acceptance verdicts or calibrated floors. D8–D11 use PASS / BORDERLINE / FAIL to mean good / mixed / poor practice. A boundary move changes the illustrated dimension; it does not rescue a failed confirmation output. Counts, where present, describe examples only.

## D1 Target lock

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**
> Target: Mara Vale, *Glass Orchard*, standard digital edition, catalogue GO-01.  
> The expanded edition GO-02 is discussed only in the separately identified edition comparison.

The note’s claims and source rows consistently identify their edition.

**Verdict reason:** Target lock is correct. The presence of another edition does not itself create ambiguity when claims remain attached to the correct objects.

**Boundary move:** Remove the catalogue distinction from a passage whose source uses the same title for both editions, while explicitly flagging the resulting ambiguity.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**
> Target: standard digital edition GO-01. The archive calls this “original version”; its catalogue identifier matches GO-01. I retain both labels because the archive’s edition terminology is unclear.

All consequential claims are tied to GO-01; the uncertainty concerns the naming convention.

**Verdict reason:** Correct with disclosed ambiguity. It straddles **use / use after fixes**: an owner could accept the identifier-based distinction or request clearer terminology. No central factual claim remains unresolved in this example.

**Boundary move:** Explain that “original version” is the archive’s label for the identified target, without implying a separately established edition.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**
> Requested target: standard digital edition GO-01.  
> This note instead covers expanded edition GO-02. Its bonus-track credits and sequencing are presented below.

The substitution is explicit throughout; the statements about GO-02 are supported.

**Verdict reason:** The researched target is wrong. This is not G1 because the substitution is disclosed, and not G4 because the note does not falsely identify GO-02 as GO-01.

**Boundary move:** Replace the substituted research with target-edition material, retaining an explicit ambiguity notice wherever edition terminology still needs clarification.

**Not this dimension:** Correctly targeting GO-01 but substituting “producer” for its row’s “additional production” belongs to D6.

**Annotator note:** “Correct with disclosed ambiguity” is not automatically inadequate. Consequential unresolved identity questions additionally invoke §2 deferral.

## D2 Accuracy

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**
> The interview was published on 14 May. Vale describes revising the lyrics after the initial recording sessions, not before them.

The preserved page supports both propositions, including temporal order.

**Verdict reason:** The atomic factual claims are supported; no temporal or negative inversion appears. Precision uses the §2 assessable-claim denominator.

**Boundary move:** Change the incidental publication date to 15 May while leaving the account of the recording sequence correct.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**
> Interview published 15 May; lyrics revised after the initial recording sessions.

The page says 14 May. The publication date is incidental: it does not establish release chronology or affect any research decision.

**Verdict reason:** The date is *imprecise*, counts as an error in precision, and is separately reported. This straddles **use / use after fixes**, depending on whether the owner requires correction before using the otherwise supported material.

**Boundary move:** Correct the date to 14 May.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**

An optional source-background paragraph misstates several incidental publication dates and reverses which of two publications changed its website name first. The album evidence itself remains supported. Inspection establishes that these details do not affect the critic’s account, interpretation or research decisions.

**Verdict reason:** The assessable claims include imprecise dates and an incorrect temporal ordering, producing poor accuracy in the inspected material. Severity is assigned using the frozen manual, not invented here. This is not G4 because the incorrect claims are non-central by consequence, not merely by placement.

**Boundary move:** Correct the paragraph’s errors except for one incidental date slip, yielding the borderline pattern.

**Not this dimension:** A recoverable, accurate claim whose supporting passage takes excessive effort to locate belongs to D4.

**Annotator note:** Unassessable claims are not errors in the precision denominator. An unresolved central or high-risk claim requires deferral, not an inferred accuracy FAIL.

## D3 Attribution

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**
> The profile writer describes the sessions as hurried. Vale’s quoted statement concerns revising lyrics; she does not call the sessions hurried.  
> The syndicated version reproduces this profile and supplies no independent corroboration.

**Verdict reason:** Speaker classes are accurate, narration is separated from creator speech, and the source relationship is correctly identified.

**Boundary move:** Keep the speaker distinction but list the syndicated copy without identifying its dependence.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**
> Sources: *Studio Ledger* profile; *Evening Arts* profile.  
> The profile writer describes the sessions as hurried.

The two entries are the same syndicated article. The note does not explicitly claim independent corroboration.

**Verdict reason:** Speaker attribution is sound, but the source presentation leaves independence unclear. This straddles **use / use after fixes**.

**Boundary move:** Identify the second entry as a syndicated copy of the first.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**
> The publication-date detail is independently corroborated by *Studio Ledger*, *Evening Arts* and the label’s press archive.

All three reproduce the same dated article. The publication date is genuinely incidental and is itself correct.

**Verdict reason:** This is an independence error: repeated publication is represented as independent evidence. It is not G5 because no material is upgraded to creator speech. The peripheral support claim also does not trigger central G9.

**Boundary move:** Remove “independently corroborated,” leaving the entries to be clarified as in the borderline example.

**Not this dimension:** A central passage of journalist narration presented as Vale’s own statement is G5, not merely a low D3 score.

**Annotator note:** An accurately attributed review is direct evidence of that reviewer’s judgment. Its relayed biographical information needs separate attribution.

## D4 Traceability

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**
> Vale on lyric revision: *Studio Ledger*, “Making Glass Orchard,” section “Second drafts,” paragraph beginning “After the sessions…”  
> Excerpt: “I rewrote the words after we had recorded the backing tracks.”

A non-producer reader recovers the passage directly; the excerpt is faithful.

**Verdict reason:** Central passage recovery succeeds with little locating work, and excerpt fidelity is established.

**Boundary move:** Remove the section and paragraph locator, retaining the article title and source link.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**
> Vale says she revised lyrics after recording the backing tracks. [*Studio Ledger*: “Making Glass Orchard”]

The source is a long, accessible interview. A non-producer reader finds the supporting passage by searching several plausible terms.

**Verdict reason:** Recovery succeeds, but the note transfers avoidable locating work to the reader. This straddles **use / use after fixes**.

**Boundary move:** Add the passage locator or a faithful searchable excerpt.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**
> Recording process: lyrics were revised after backing tracks were recorded. Sources: bibliography.

The bibliography contains long interviews and recordings without claim-to-source mapping, timestamps or passage locators. A non-producer recovery attempt requires reading across the collection; eventual recovery establishes support.

**Verdict reason:** Passage recovery and time-to-locate are poor despite supported claims. This is not G2 or G9: the sources exist and establish the claims. Established invented quotations would instead trigger G2.

**Boundary move:** Attach each central claim to its particular source, leaving only within-source locating work.

**Not this dimension:** A perfectly located passage that does not establish the asserted proposition belongs to D2 and potentially G9.

**Annotator note:** If missing preservation prevents adjudication, apply the contract’s unassessable rules. Do not treat unavailable evidence as merely inconvenient navigation.

## D5 Provenance communication

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**
> Vale’s signed statement, read in the preserved original, describes revising the lyrics.  
> A profile relays the engineer’s account of the recording order; the original engineer interview was not retrieved.

**Verdict reason:** Wording communicates primary and relayed access claim by claim, without requiring formal labels.

**Boundary move:** Replace “the original engineer interview was not retrieved” with an unexplained uncertainty marker that leaves the relay’s status unclear.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**
> The profile reports the engineer’s account of the recording order. Status: uncertain.

The profile is preserved and explicit, but the note does not explain whether “uncertain” concerns the relay, the underlying account or a conflicting source.

**Verdict reason:** Provenance communication is partly adequate but unresolved uncertainty is poorly specified. This straddles **use / use after fixes**.

**Boundary move:** State that the relay was read and the original interview was not retrieved; identify any actual conflict separately.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**
> Whether Vale discussed lyric revision remains unconfirmed; treat this as a speculative lead.

The note’s evidence record contains a directly retrieved, signed statement explicitly discussing the revision, and the attribution has been adjudicated as supported.

**Verdict reason:** This consequentially understates available evidence and leaves an answered research question apparently unresolved. It is not G9: no unsupported positive claim is presented as verified. There is no false claim of reading.

**Boundary move:** Acknowledge the signed statement while retaining a vague status marker, producing the narrower ambiguity in the borderline example.

**Not this dimension:** Saying “I read the original interview” when evidence establishes that this is false is G3b.

**Annotator note:** Primary access is not a guarantee that every statement is true. Classify the evidence route for the particular claim.

## D6 Credits and editions

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**
> GO-01, track “Window”: Ivo Chen — “additional production.”  
> Authority: label booklet, GO-01, track-credit row “Window.”  
> Chen has this role on “Window” and “Steps”: 2 of the edition’s 8 tracks; bonus tracks excluded.

The rows and aggregate agree.

**Verdict reason:** Role, track and edition are precise against named authoritative rows; the aggregate supplies role, numerator, denominator, exclusions and source.

**Boundary move:** Retain the exact row but introduce an unexplained shorthand, “production,” in the summary.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**
> Summary: Chen — production on “Window.”  
> Exact row: “additional production,” GO-01 booklet, “Window.”

**Verdict reason:** The evidence is available, but the summary can be read as substituting a broader role. This straddles **use / use after fixes**. The source does not establish that Chen lacked other production involvement, so the example does not establish a central incorrect claim.

**Boundary move:** Use “additional production” consistently.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**
> Credit worksheet — unverified possibilities, not established credits:  
> “Window”: Chen may be producer; “Steps”: Chen may be mixer. Edition match not checked.

The available, named GO-01 booklet rows instead specify “additional production” and “recording engineer.” The worksheet never extracts them correctly.

**Verdict reason:** The note fails to deliver role, track and edition precision against accessible authoritative rows. The suggestions are explicitly unverified, so this is not G4 or G9. No numeric aggregate is used, so G7 is absent. An incomplete aggregate used as a central premise would trigger G7.

**Boundary move:** Replace the worksheet with the correct edition-specific rows, leaving only the summary shorthand illustrated above.

**Not this dimension:** An undisclosed switch to GO-02 in central claims is G1.

**Annotator note:** An unverified label does not make a credit usable. Here the authoritative rows resolve the audit; this is not an unresolved high-risk claim.

## D7 Omissions

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**
> Vale identifies “Window” as the track carrying the family vocals; she describes them as backing vocals, not a featured lead.

The frozen reference-map opportunity asks which track and which vocal role. The accessible interview supports both.

**Verdict reason:** The opportunity is captured because substance and scope are correctly extracted. Relevant initial and amended map accounting remains separate.

**Boundary move:** Omit the track identification while expressly recording it as a gap.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**
> Vale discusses family backing vocals. Track identification remains a gap in this note.

The available interview identifies “Window”; the note captures the role but not the question’s full scope.

**Verdict reason:** This is a disclosed gap, not a captured opportunity. It straddles **use / use after fixes** when the owner could use the remaining material while resolving the named gap.

**Boundary move:** Extract the track identification from the interview and attach it to the vocal-role statement.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**

The note supplies general biography but says nothing about the family-vocal question. That question belongs to the frozen top-3, and its answer is present in an accessible, retrieved interview.

**Verdict reason:** This is a silent essential miss; substance and scope are not captured. It is not G6 because the note makes no claim that the vocals or participation do not exist. Turning failed discovery into a central assertion of non-involvement would trigger G6.

**Boundary move:** Explicitly disclose the missing answer, reaching the borderline pattern without improving recall.

**Not this dimension:** “No family member participated,” inferred centrally from an unsuccessful search, is G6.

**Annotator note:** A disclosed gap is still uncaptured. Inaccessible essentials leave the recall denominator but remain reported for task sufficiency.

## D8 Musical context

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**
> In her signed statement, Vale names fictional artist Neri Sol’s use of overlapping refrains as an influence on “Window.”  
> Listening inquiry: compare how refrain overlap functions; the source does not establish that the recordings achieve the same effect.

The owner records that this helped plan a listening question.

**Verdict reason:** The lead has album-specific purpose, supported influence status and a concrete comparison reason without prescribing a conclusion.

**Boundary move:** Retain the supported influence attribution but remove the specific refrain-based listening inquiry.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**
> Vale names Neri Sol as an influence on “Window.” Compare their work.

The source supports the influence; the owner finds the lead potentially useful but must formulate its musical purpose.

**Verdict reason:** Relationship type and support are sound, but the comparison lacks a concrete reason. This straddles **use / use after fixes** as descriptive practice.

**Boundary move:** Add the source-grounded refrain relationship and an open listening question.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**
> Possible critic’s thesis: this album proves Vale has surpassed her influences. Build the review around that verdict.

This is proposed argumentation, with no claimed listening experience or assertion that a source established the judgment.

**Verdict reason:** The note presupposes the critic’s conclusion rather than supplying a supported contextual lead. This is poor D8 practice, not G3a: it makes no own-observation or listening claim.

**Boundary move:** Replace the verdict with the documented influence and a still-general comparison invitation.

**Not this dimension:** “On listening, I heard overlapping refrains,” without the permitted, claimed audio access, is G3a.

**Annotator note:** An attributed critic’s judgment is admissible reception evidence; endorsing it as the note’s finding is different.

## D9 Selection

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**

The main note contains distinct evidence units addressing release identity, credits and creator statements. The appendix adds source passages needed for verification. It does not restate each claim as fresh content.

**Verdict reason:** Inspected substantive units serve research or verification purposes; selection is assessed across the entire deliverable.

**Boundary move:** Add a closing recap that repeats the same claims without adding navigation or evidence value.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**

The core is useful, but a closing recap repeats its evidence nearly verbatim. The owner can skip it easily; the appendix remains useful.

**Verdict reason:** Useful and redundant units coexist. This straddles **use / use after fixes**; the decision depends on the recap’s actual reading cost and value, not a word limit.

**Boundary move:** Delete the redundant recap.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**

The same creator statement appears in the overview, evidence table, track notes, checklist and appendix without distinct functions. A long unrelated childhood chronology surrounds the album evidence.

**Verdict reason:** The whole deliverable repeatedly spends substantive units on duplication and irrelevant material. Claim splitting and appendix placement earn no selection credit.

**Boundary move:** Consolidate the repeated statement and remove the unrelated chronology, leaving the smaller redundant recap illustrated above.

**Not this dimension:** A short note that silently omits an accessible essential belongs to D7.

**Annotator note:** Repetition serving passage verification or navigation is not automatically redundant. Judge the function of each occurrence.

## D10 Collection efficiency and stopping

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**

The trace retrieves a booklet and interview, identifies an apparent edition conflict, then performs a late comparison against the label catalogue. That search resolves the conflict; collection stops after addressing the remaining research questions.

**Verdict reason:** The yield curve shows useful initial collection and consequential late checking. Wall time, calls and failures are reported; lateness alone is not waste.

**Boundary move:** Add an unchanged retry of a failed endpoint after its required evidence has already been obtained elsewhere, with no remaining question attached.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**

The trace otherwise follows the PASS pattern but includes an unchanged failed retry after an adequate alternative source was recovered. It then stops.

**Verdict reason:** There is avoidable collection work alongside productive research. This straddles **use / use after fixes**, with “fixes” concerning collection practice rather than necessarily the delivered prose.

**Boundary move:** Remove the purposeless retry.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**

The trace repeatedly issues the same request to an endpoint returning the same permanent-denial response. It then repeats equivalent searches returning already-preserved passages, with no unresolved conflict or question identified. The yield record stays flat through this sequence.

**Verdict reason:** Repeated identical failures and sustained non-yielding collection demonstrate poor efficiency and stopping. No deception is alleged: the trace accurately records the attempts.

**Boundary move:** Stop the non-yielding sequence after the isolated unnecessary retry illustrated above.

**Not this dimension:** Following retrieved page instructions to abandon the album task is G8.

**Annotator note:** Without traces, D10 is N/A, not FAIL. Confirming searches can yield confidence or conflict resolution without adding a new claim.

## D11 Owner burden

### PASS

**Observable behaviour**

The owner’s task record includes reading, a targeted source check, independent completion work and production of the evidence-backed outline with unresolved questions recorded. All active work is counted. Evaluation-only audit work is separately logged. The paired baseline required more active work.

**Verdict reason:** The observed task reaches the common endpoint with lower total active burden. This is good D11 performance, not by itself a time-saving benefit verdict under §8.

**Boundary move:** Add enough avoidable source-location and correction work that the task’s apparent saving is substantially eroded, yielding the mixed pattern below.

### BORDERLINE

**Observable behaviour**

The note reduces independent searching, but the owner spends that saved effort locating passages and repairing a confusing credit summary. The complete record shows little overall difference from the paired baseline; the endpoint is reached.

**Verdict reason:** Burden is redistributed rather than clearly reduced. This straddles **use / use after fixes** as descriptive practice; useful evidence may justify use despite the repair cost.

**Boundary move:** Remove the recorded locating and correction work by making the relevant passages and credit distinctions usable at first reading.

### FAIL

**Observable behaviour**

The owner reads the note, reconstructs its evidence links, corrects its worksheets and independently fills its gaps before reaching the endpoint. The complete task record shows greater active work than the paired baseline, attributable to these note-related tasks.

**Verdict reason:** The deliverable imposes substantial R, V, F and completion work instead of reducing total owner burden. This does not require a gate failure.

**Boundary move:** Eliminate the reconstruction and correction work until the remaining completion work produces the mixed burden pattern above.

**Not this dimension:** Unattended producer collection latency belongs to D10 and elapsed-availability reporting; it enters D11 only when it requires owner work.

**Annotator note:** Necessary re-verification during the task counts. Evaluation-only auditing is separate even when it examines the same sources.

## Contract friction

- **D7 requires the reference map.** Note, trace and sources alone cannot establish essential recall or frozen-top-3 misses. These anchors require the preserved initial and amended maps, frozen-top-3 designation and run-time accessibility findings specified by §5. Inferring essentials afresh would change the contract.
- **D8 and D11 require owner records.** D8’s usefulness component cannot be inferred solely from textual quality. D11 requires the complete task record; comparative burden additionally needs the paired baseline. The examples supply those observations rather than treating model opinion as owner judgment.
- **The anchors cannot supply calibrated boundaries.** D1’s categorical statuses do not mechanically map to use decisions, and D2 severity definitions remain for the scoring manual. D8–D11 remain descriptive unless subsequently bound. The boundary moves illustrate neighbouring behaviours, not numeric or universally binding cutoffs.
- **Unassessable is not a fourth performance rung to disguise.** Where evidence cannot resolve central/high-risk claims or gate allegations, the specified deferral rules apply. These examples cannot convert that procedural outcome into a dimension FAIL.

## Self-check

- **D1 BORDERLINE:** Least confident because disclosed ambiguity can coexist with fully adequate target lock. The example limits uncertainty to terminology so it does not conceal a central unresolved identity claim.
- **D6 FAIL:** Explicitly provisional wrong credit candidates isolate poor credit extraction from G4/G9, but this is a narrower failure pattern than confident role substitution. Calibration should test whether owners distinguish it reliably.
- **D10 BORDERLINE:** Collection defects may leave a fully usable note. Its use/use-after-fixes pair concerns descriptive process practice and must not become an output-rejection rule without §6 calibration.
- **D11 PASS:** Lower observed burden is clear, but matched albums remain different. The anchor describes the recorded outcome and does not establish causation or the separate §8 benefit verdict.
