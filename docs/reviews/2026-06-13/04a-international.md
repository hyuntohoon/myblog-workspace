## Cross-outlet synthesis (for the blog's own design)

- **Scores are the exception, not the rule.** Of the 5, only **Pitchfork** publishes a numeric score (0.0–10.0 + Best New Music badge). The Quietus, Bandcamp Daily, Hearing Things, and First Floor are **all score-free / essayistic**. This is corroborating context for the workspace's own "album rating only, 0–5/0.5, stars-only" decision (project-feature-roadmap) — a numeric/star score is a *minority* convention among respected critical outlets, used mainly by the aggregation-feeder (Pitchfork→Metacritic) tier.
- **Discovery = lists + "of the day/week/month" cadence + year-end.** Every active outlet leans on recurring curated rails (Album of the Day, Essential Releases, Music of the Month, Five Albums, year-end lists) far more than on a searchable score database. A blog wanting "discovery" should prioritize editorial list features over a ratings leaderboard.
- **Business model is splitting away from ads toward reader-funding.** Pitchfork just added a $5/mo paywall (Jan 2026); The Quietus and Hearing Things are membership/worker-owned; First Floor is Substack paid. Bandcamp Daily is the outlier — pure commerce-subsidized free editorial. Ad-only is no longer the default for serious music criticism in 2026.
- **The "Substack newsletter" model is real but churns.** Two of the task's suggested examples (Music Journalism Insider, Penny Fractions) are already dead; the durable ones (First Floor, Hearing Things) are author/worker-owned with a clear paid tier. Longevity tracks with direct reader revenue, not free-only.
