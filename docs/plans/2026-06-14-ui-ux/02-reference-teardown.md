# 02 — Reference / Competitor IA Teardown

**Scope.** A citation-backed IA + UX teardown of seven reference sites across the personal-log ↔ editorial-authority spectrum: **Letterboxd**, **Rate Your Music (RYM/Sonemic)**, **Album of the Year (AOTY)**, **Pitchfork**, **IZM**, **음악취향Y (Music Y)**, and **Bandcamp Daily**. For each site we cover (1) what the home/front page shows first and curates by, (2) IA / navigation + core page types, (3) where it sits on the personal-log ↔ editorial-authority axis and the *mechanism* by which it makes records public, and (4) how the review/record unit is displayed (score / length / structure) plus discovery and subscribe surfaces.

**Relationship to prior art.** This builds on `docs/reviews/2026-06-13/04a-international.md` and `04b-domestic.md`. Those were **outlet-survey-framed** (who covers what, is it active, funding model). This pass refreshes the same and adjacent sites through an **information-architecture + bucket-vs-magazine lens** — i.e. page-type taxonomy, nav structure, and the specific machinery each site uses to turn a private/individual record into a public-facing one. It also adds three sites **never previously torn down** (Letterboxd, RYM, AOTY were only ever used as *mirrors* in `04-market-research.md`, never analyzed).

**Evidence rule.** Every code claim cites `file_path:line`; every external claim cites a source URL; anything unverifiable is marked *(speculation)* or *(unverified)*. **Hard fetch wall:** several reference sites are aggressively bot-hostile — `letterboxd.com`, `rateyourmusic.com`, `albumoftheyear.org`, and `pitchfork.com` all return **HTTP 403 to WebFetch (and to `curl` with a real User-Agent) on every path tried** (plus, for Pitchfork, the Wayback Machine, TV Tropes, and Axios also 403'd). Claims about those four sites' live pages are therefore reconstructed from (a) WebSearch result-snippets quoting the sites' own copy, (b) third-party write-ups / UX case studies, and (c) Wikipedia. Live-page-only details are marked inline. The two Korean sites (`izm.co.kr`, `musicy.kr`) and Bandcamp Daily were directly fetchable (Music Y via `curl` over HTTP only — see its section).

### Letterboxd

**Status / method.** Fresh teardown (no Letterboxd analysis exists in `04a`/`04b`). **Blocker:** every `letterboxd.com` URL (home, `/welcome/`, `/about/faq/`, `/journal/*`, `/about/pro/`) returned **HTTP 403 to WebFetch**. Claims rest on WebSearch snippets quoting Letterboxd's own copy and third-party write-ups (TapSmart, alittlebithuman, fivestarinsider, a UX redesign spec). Live-page-only details are marked *(unverified)*/*(speculation)*.

**(1) HOME / front page.**
- Logged-OUT, the front page is a **discovery shopfront**: "popular movies of the week and what your friends are watching," plus "a selection of popular films, reviews and lists from Letterboxd members"; doubles as the DB browse entry (by decade/year, genre, popularity, rating, streaming service). Source: https://letterboxd.com/welcome/ ; https://letterboxd.com/ ; https://letterboxd.com/films/popular/
- Logged-IN, the same surface **personalizes to the user's network** — "as you follow more people, the page personalizes to show what's popular in your network," plus an **Activity Feed** of "friends' recent reviews and lists." Source: https://letterboxd.com/ (snippet).
- **Curation axis = recency-weighted popularity + social proximity**, NOT editorial selection. Named home rails: Popular films of the week, Popular reviews (this week), Popular lists (this week). "Highest Rated"/Top-500 canon is an **algorithmic average of member ratings with a 25,000-rating eligibility floor** (no editor in the loop). Source: https://letterboxd.com/official/list/letterboxds-top-500-films/ ; https://nofilmschool.com/letterboxd-top-500-films
- *(unverified)* exact above-the-fold module order on the live logged-out page — assembled from snippets, not a rendered page (403).

**(2) IA / navigation + core page types.**
- **Primary nav (live app):** Home, **Search** (magnifier), **Profile** (person), and an activity/Feed surface (the "lightning-bolt" icon, which a UX spec flags as unlabeled and proposes relabeling "Feed"). Source (third-party spec): https://matt-sutherland.com/project/letterboxd-spec — the relabel is the author's proposal, not shipped; *(speculation)* that Letterboxd itself uses the word "Feed."
- **Core page types:**
  - **Film page** — one canonical page per film: poster/backdrop, synopsis, cast/crew, genres, a **ratings bar (score + distribution histogram)**, member **reviews sortable by popularity / rating / date**; actions to rate, like, log, add-to-watchlist, add-to-list. Source: https://www.tapsmart.com/features/letterboxd-deep-dive/
  - **Member profile / collection page** — "recent activity" up top, then tabs: **Films, Diary, Reviews, Lists, Tags, Stats**; reached by clicking your username; you can **pin** lists/diary/reviews via a "profile" tag. Source: https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/ (snippet); https://officiallouise.medium.com/a-tour-of-my-insane-letterboxd-profile-just-for-you-dcbfd2b34678
  - **Diary page** — chronological log of "when you saw each film, alongside its rating." Source: TapSmart; FAQ snippet.
  - **List page** — user-built ordered/unordered collections, each entry with optional notes + spoiler flag, drag-to-sort, tag-browsable; a first-class, shareable content type with its own popularity ranking. Source: https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/ (snippet); https://letterboxd.com/journal/hit-list-new-stats-for-lists-feature/ (title only — page 403).
  - **Review page** — attached to a film+user; supports spoiler-gating, likes, comment-visibility controls. Source: TapSmart.
  - *(unverified)* exact tab set/order on the live profile (403).

**(3) Axis position + public-record mechanism.** **Firmly personal-log end; authority emerges bottom-up from aggregation, not top-down editorial.** Self-description: "**Social film discovery**." The mechanism:
  1. **Logging is the atomic act.** Marking a film watched + rating + optional "like" + watch-date simultaneously builds your **Diary** AND emits a **public Activity event** (also fills profile "Recent Activity"). Source: https://www.tapsmart.com/features/letterboxd-deep-dive/ ; FAQ snippet.
  2. **Follow graph turns logs into a feed.** Following members streams their watches/reviews/lists into your Activity Feed — personal logs become other people's discovery surface. Source: https://letterboxd.com/ (snippet).
  3. **Aggregation manufactures "authority" without editors.** Thousands of half-star ratings roll up into the film-page **distribution histogram** and the **Top-500 average-rating canon** (25k-rating floor). Source: https://letterboxd.com/official/list/letterboxds-top-500-films/
  4. **Every profile is syndicated.** "Every member profile has an **RSS feed** of new diary entries, reviews and lists" — the private log is machine-readable/publishable by default (auto-publishable via IFTTT). Source: https://github.com/zactopus/letterboxd ; https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/ (snippet).
- **Catch for myblog:** the authority mechanism needs many users; a single author has no crowd to aggregate, so it can borrow Letterboxd's **log→public-record→RSS plumbing** but NOT its crowd-aggregation authority source.

**(4) The review/record UNIT.**
- **Score:** **0.5–5 stars, 0.5 increments.** Half-stars are deliberately **un-standardized** — "a 3.5/5 will mean different things to different people," encouraging calibration to a *specific person* rather than a universal scale. Most common rating = **4 stars / 80%** (~a quarter of all ratings). Source: https://razzledazzlecr.com/blogs/articles/letterbox-ratings ; https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/movie-reviews-rating-scales
- **Rating decoupled from review:** you can rate + "like" with **zero prose** — star + like is the minimal record; written review is optional. Source: https://www.tapsmart.com/features/letterboxd-deep-dive/
- **Length/structure:** **no template, no minimum** — one-line jokes to long essays; each carries the star rating, optional spoiler-gate, like count, comment-visibility control. *(speculation)* on a measured word-count norm (403).
- **Discovery:** Discover (Popular This Week / Highest Rated); home Popular reviews / Popular lists rails; Lists as a first-class tag-browsable surface; film-page review sort; browse by decade/genre/streaming-service; algorithmic Top-500 canon. Source: https://letterboxd.com/ ; https://letterboxd.com/films/popular/
- **Subscribe/RSS/email:** **Per-profile RSS** of new diary/reviews/lists + full data **export**. **Year in Review** recap + year-round **Stats** are **paid** — **Pro $19/yr** (streaming filters, watchlist notifications, activity filters, ad-free) and **Patron $49/yr** (custom posters/backdrops, above/below-community-average stats, Themes/Nanogenres, early access). Sources: https://github.com/zactopus/letterboxd ; https://alittlebithuman.com/is-letterboxd-pro-or-patron-worth-it/ ; https://www.fivestarinsider.com/letterboxd-pro-vs-patron/ ; https://letterboxd.com/journal/2025-letterboxd-year-in-review-faq/ (title only — 403).

**Axis-position verdict:** Far personal-log end — a diary-first social log whose "editorial authority" is entirely **emergent from aggregating + ranking + syndicating** thousands of personal records (RSS-public-by-default, algorithmic canon, no staff verdicts). The lesson for a single-author bucket site: copy the **log→public-record→RSS pipeline and the free-form-review-with-optional-half-star unit**, NOT the crowd-aggregation authority engine.

### Rate Your Music (RYM / Sonemic)

**Status / method.** Fresh teardown (not in `04a`/`04b`). **Access caveat:** `rateyourmusic.com` returns **403 to WebFetch on every path** (homepage, `/charts/`, `/new-music/`). Live-page claims anchored to (a) Wikipedia, (b) RYM's own wiki/feature/subscribe pages via WebSearch snippets, (c) a third-party UX case study walking the actual DOM. Unanchored items = *speculation*. **Scale:** ~1.3M users, 6.6M releases, 147M ratings (Mar 2025, Wikipedia); homepage counters cite higher (1.9M artists / 7.3M releases / 172M ratings / 3.76M reviews / 819k+ lists). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_Your_Music ; https://rateyourmusic.com/ (snippet).

**(1) HOME / front page.**
- **Community-activity-led, not editorially curated.** A UX teardown of the live page: "the largest element on the page… happens to be a user review of [an album]" — the dominant above-the-fold block is a surfaced **member review**, and the page "lacks a call to action." Source: https://medium.com/@justicedsn/rateyourmusic-case-study-an-examination-of-ryms-ux-flaws-dca7f724462b (FETCHED).
- Ranking driver = **community charts** ("the greatest albums of 2026, voted by RYM/Sonemic users"), a **new-music rail**, and a periodic editorial **"Popular in [year]"** feature. Sources: https://rateyourmusic.com/ , https://rateyourmusic.com/charts/ (snippets).
- The one genuinely **editorial** homepage element — **"Rate Your Music: Popular in 2026"** ("top releases the RYM community is listening to … with member reviews," + curated playlist, published Apr 19 2026) — is still *aggregated community taste*, not a critic verdict. Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/feature/rate-your-music-popular-in-2026/ (snippet).
- Top nav (per teardown): a **search** field + section tabs, a **`$`** icon (subscription) and **`[X]`** icon (logout) — flagged as opaque iconography. Source: Medium case study (FETCHED).

**(2) IA / navigation + core page types.**
- **Release page** — the catalog spine ("sorted by release and issue"): aggregate weighted score, genres/secondary genres, descriptors/tags, tracklist (with per-track ratings), review stream. Layout called "quite good." Sources: Medium case study (FETCHED); https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/ (snippet).
- **Charts** (`/charts/…`) — ranked leaderboards by year/decade/all-time, filterable by genre + release type; e.g. `/charts/top/album/all-time/`, `/charts/popular/album/2026/`. Sources: https://rateyourmusic.com/charts/ , https://rateyourmusic.com/charts/popular/album/2026/ (snippets).
- **New music** (`/new-music/`) — recently-released + upcoming browse. *(layout = speculation, 403.)* Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/new-music/ (snippet).
- **Profile / collection page** — per-user catalog: pictures, bio, **friends list, shoutbox, private messages**, full rating catalog, lists, an "artist map." Teardown notes friends are buried "far down" and calls the artist map "entirely unnecessary." Sources: Medium case study (FETCHED); https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/ (snippet).
- **Lists** (`/list/…`) — user rankings ("debuts, influential albums, annual bests," micro-genre canons); landing surfaces popular + recently-updated. Sources: Medium case study (FETCHED); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_Your_Music
- **Genres** — maintained genre tree/wiki (hierarchical genres + descriptors) powering genre-filtered charts + recommendations. Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/Music:Genres (snippet).
- **Discover** (`/discover`) — recommendation surface driven by your own ratings. Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/discover (snippet).
- **Message boards** — forums per genre/topic. Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/ (snippet).

**(3) Axis position + public-record mechanism.** **Far personal-log / collective-catalog pole — no central editorial voice at all.** Authority is manufactured purely by aggregation of private logs:
- Each member's action is a **private cataloging act** (rate 0.5–5.0, tag, log playcounts/notes, optionally review). Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/ ; Wikipedia.
- Private ratings become public **authority** via a **Bayesian-weighted chart algorithm**: each user gets a **weighting 0–100%** (typical 0 / 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.25) from activity, rating diversity, recency, plus a **review-writing bonus (up to ~25%)**; inactive votes decay; formula deliberately secret; artist charts require a **1,000-rating cutoff**. Sources: https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/RYM:Weighting%20FAQ , https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/RYM:Charts+FAQ (snippets); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_average
- So "Best albums of all time" is **literally an aggregate of personal collections, weighted by curator credibility** — the public-record mechanism solved at *crowd* scale. **Single-author lesson:** RYM proves a personal-log unit (rating + catalog entry) *can become* the public/editorial layer without a magazine voice — but only with millions of logs to average. A solo site borrows the *unit* (rate + tag + short note as the atomic public record) and must supply authority through *voice* instead of weighting. (Analysis, not a sourced claim.)

**(4) The review/record UNIT.**
- **Score:** **0.5–5.0 stars, 0.5 increments** — identical to myblog's decided album scale. Score is primary and omnipresent; a rating can exist with **no prose**; release page shows the community weighted average prominently. Sources: Wikipedia ; https://rateyourmusic.com/list/Kankato/my-rating-system/ ; https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/ (snippets).
- **Length/structure:** highly variable — one-liners to long-form essays "covering history, production, and musical analysis." Formatting uses **forum-style BBCode, not Markdown**; review titles render as "plain bold text … nothing between it and the content except a line break" — **minimal, unstyled review chrome.** Sources: https://writeseen.com/blog/rate-your-music ; Medium case study (FETCHED). *(Typical word count = speculation, 403.)*
- **Discovery:** (a) rating-driven personal recommendations + `/discover`; (b) genre/tag/descriptor browse; (c) charts (year/decade/all-time/genre); (d) friend/follow graph (add via `+`, custom friend charts, collection comparison, notifications); (e) user lists as a canon/discovery layer. Sources: https://rateyourmusic.com/discover ; https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/ (snippets).
- **Subscribe/RSS/email:** **Weekly email newsletter** ("a weekly roundup of new releases, special features, community posts, and music news"). Paid **RYM Ultimate** (`/subscribe`): daily chart updates (vs weekly free), double-length connected-streaming playlists, supporter-only boards, early access to editorial features, IP-block bypass; ads partly opt-in (users can voluntarily enable ads to fund the site). **Native RSS = unverified** (no RSS evidence in any fetched source). Sources: https://rateyourmusic.com/subscribe/ , https://rateyourmusic.com/discover , https://rateyourmusic.com/development/ (snippets).

**Axis-position verdict:** Pure **personal-log / collective-catalog** extreme — zero house critical voice; public authority manufactured entirely by **Bayesian-weighting millions of private ratings into charts**. For myblog the takeaway is the *unit and the public-record mechanism* (0.5–5 star + tags + optional note as the atomic public bucket entry), not the aggregation engine.

**(1) HOME / front page.**

Source: https://www.albumoftheyear.org/

**(3) Axis position + public-record mechanism.** **Community-aggregation / database pole — the opposite end from this blog's single-author editorial intent.** No first-party editorial voice: critic scores are scraped from *other* outlets, converted to a percentage, and averaged; its own content is the *aggregate* plus a crowd. The **mechanism making personal records public** is the **profile-as-rating-database**: every user rates 0–100, and those ratings (a) build a public, browsable, CSV-exportable personal collection page AND (b) **roll up into the site-wide "User Score"** sitting next to the Critic Score on every album page. A private listening log becomes public *twice* — as a profile artifact and as a vote in a global leaderboard. **This is exactly the model the workspace already ruled out** (community votes/members dropped; "no fake community data"). AOTY's whole value proposition is the crowd.

**(4) The review/record UNIT.**
- **Score:** two prominent numbers on a **0–100 scale** per album — aggregate **Critic Score** (with review count) and aggregate **User Score** (with rating count), color-banded. AOTY **does not write reviews** — the unit is a *score row with linked excerpts*, not an essay; outlet grades on any native scale are **converted to a percentage and averaged**. Source: https://www.albumoftheyear.org/ (snippet on score colors); methodology snippet (~since 2008).
- **User-review unit:** capsule-to-long, optionally with **per-track numeric ratings**; review pages toggle the rating distribution between **"All Users" and "Following"** and support comments. Source: https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/55576-twice-page-two/user-reviews/ , https://www.albumoftheyear.org/user-reviews/
- **Discovery:** Must Hear (threshold rail), **"Under the Radar"** (25–150 user ratings, user score ≥80, ≥1 week old, released within 9 months), Popular Now, Highly Anticipated/Upcoming, genre + release-type filters, user **Lists** (`/lists/users/`), Year-End hub, **publication leaderboards** mirroring each outlet. Source: https://www.albumoftheyear.org/discover/ , /must-hear/
- **Subscribe/RSS/email:** **RSS feeds exist** at `/rss/`. Paid **Subscribe** (`/subscribe/`) = a **one-time annual payment** for a **subscriber badge + ad-free + "more benefits"**; snippet states **~2,411 active subscribers** *(unverified — WebSearch-snippet only; subscribe page 403)*. No first-party editorial newsletter surfaced. *(speculation — exact price/full benefits; subscribe page 403.)*

**Axis-position verdict:** Pure **community-aggregation database at the far crowd / borrowed-authority pole** — the inverse of a single-author editorial publication; its public-record mechanism (profile ratings → site-wide User Score leaderboard) is precisely the community-vote model the bucket-vs-magazine blog has already deliberately ruled out. The one transferable idea — AOTY's score-threshold **"Must Hear" rail** as a cheap credibility filter — collapses, for a single author, into the editor's own taste, i.e. the BNM/editorial-pick model already in `FEAT-home-redesign`, so it adds nothing new.

### Pitchfork

**Status / method.** Refreshes `04a-international.md` §1 (lines 15–39), which was blocked on pitchfork.com (403) and marked home layout/RSS/review structure as guess/unverified. This pass **confirms the RSS feeds (now verified), the IA/URL structure, and the new 2026 reader-score mechanism**. The 403 wall persists and now also blocks Wayback, TV Tropes, and Axios — so the **exact above-the-fold layout and current review word-count remain speculation.**

**(1) HOME / front page.**
- **Editorially curated, not algorithmically ranked.** **"Best New Music" (BNM) is the marquee front-page slot** — BNM music "is featured on the front page of the site." The site is "best known for its daily output of music reviews" and "publishes best-of lists … and annual features and retrospectives each year." Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) ; https://ahrefs.com/websites/pitchfork.com/competitors
- Curation logic = recency + editorial judgment: a daily stream, highest-scoring/most-significant releases elevated to BNM and pinned up top, alongside Features and News; **no user-popularity ranking** drives the home. **Rail-order specifics are speculation** (pitchfork.com, web.archive.org, tvtropes.org all 403), inferred from the section/RSS taxonomy.

**(2) IA / navigation + core page types.** Verified via Pitchfork's own RSS taxonomy (https://rss.feedspot.com/pitchfork_rss_feeds/) and review-index paths:
- **Reviews** — `/reviews/albums/` (the core unit) + **Track Reviews** (`/feed/feed-track-reviews/rss`); highest-volume page type (28,000+ reviews by 2021; 30,000+ archive cited at the 2026 paywall — https://exclaim.ca/music/article/pitchfork-paywalls-reviews-launches-subscription-service).
- **Best New Music hub** — `/best/` with **Best New Albums** (`/reviews/best/albums/`), **Best New Reissues** (`/reviews/best/reissues/`), **Best New Tracks** (`/reviews/best/tracks/`).
- **Features**, **News**, **The Pitch** (music-culture column), **Columns**, **Lists & Guides** (`/features/lists-and-guides/`) — each with its own RSS feed.
- **Recurring columns:** **Sunday Review** (weekly retrospective of a significant past album, since 2016), **Rising** (emerging-artist profiles), **The Pitch**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website)
- **Page types:** (a) single-album review page, (b) list/index pages (reviews index, Best New hubs, year-end lists), (c) feature/column article pages, (d) annual **"Albums of the Year"**. There is **no user-profile/collection page** — the only account surface is the new subscriber identity (username for scoring/commenting).

**(3) Axis position + public-record mechanism.** **Far editorial-authority pole** — a masthead of staff critics, a single authoritative numeric verdict per album, the BNM "anointing" mechanism; *institutional criticism*, the opposite of a personal log. Owner = **Condé Nast** (acquired Oct 13 2015), **merged into GQ Jan 17 2024** with layoffs (Wikipedia).
- **2026 mechanism for surfacing "personal records" publicly (load-bearing):** the **Jan 20 2026 paywall** ($5/mo or $50/yr) introduced reader accounts that let subscribers **rate albums on the same 0.0–10 scale and comment**. These aggregate into a **"Reader Score" displayed directly beneath Pitchfork's official critic score** once **≥5 people have rated** — explicitly **Rotten-Tomatoes-style**. Rules: **one score per album per subscriber**, comment under a chosen username, editor-moderated. Sources: https://axios.com/2026/01/20/pitchfork-subscription-reader-scores-comments ; https://exclaim.ca/music/article/pitchfork-paywalls-reviews-launches-subscription-service ; https://thequietus.com/news/pitchfork-launches-reviews-paywall/
- **Bucket-vs-magazine lesson:** Pitchfork keeps editorial authority **primary** (critic score on top) and bolts reader records on as a **secondary, aggregated, subordinate layer** — readers get a number + comment but **no personal collection page, no public per-user library**. The individual reader's record is invisible except as an anonymized aggregate contribution. The editorial pole's answer to public personal records: **collapse them into one aggregate stat under the critic's verdict**, never a per-person showcase.

**(4) The review/record UNIT.**
- **Score:** **0.0–10.0 decimal, one decimal place** (e.g. 7.8) — distinctive vs the 5-point norm; founder Ryan Schreiber called it "scientific without any actual science." Scores cluster 6.4–7.8, mean ≈7.0; a 10.0 is "a seismic event." Score presence = always. Reader Score (0.0–10) now displays below when ≥5 ratings exist. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) ; https://nolanbconaway.github.io/blog/2017/pitchfork-roundup.html
- **BNM badge:** editorial designation on top of the score (since 2003); ~top ~5% of reviews; BNM mean ≈8.6 (https://www.nsgrantham.com/pitchfork-reviews). Separate **Best New Reissue** track.

## What a solo bucket-blog can realistically BORROW

- **The log→public-record→RSS pipeline (Letterboxd).** One write (bucket-add + optional star + optional note) should simultaneously build a private-feeling record AND emit a public, syndicated artifact, with **per-profile RSS by default**. This is single-author-safe because it needs no crowd — it's plumbing on *one* person's logs, and myblog already ships RSS. *Why it fits:* the bucket foundation IS a personal log; Letterboxd shows the log can be the public record without any editorial layer. Source: https://github.com/zactopus/letterboxd ; https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/
- **Rate-without-prose as the minimal unit (RYM + Letterboxd).** Let a bucket entry exist as just `0.5–5★ + tags + optional short note`, with a full review layered on later. The atomic public record is the rating, not the essay — this lets a near-zero-content site populate gracefully (one author can rate fast, write slowly). *Why it fits:* matches myblog's already-decided 0.5–5 album scale and degrades to near-zero published prose. Source: https://www.tapsmart.com/features/letterboxd-deep-dive/ ; https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/
- **Editor-curated canon pages as the authority surface (IZM 명반 + 연말결산; 음악취향Y Best/Choice).** A single author earns "magazine" weight not by aggregation but by maintaining **standing canon lists** (a "명반"-style 4.0+ canon tab) and **non-ranked year-end Picks** — explicitly framed as *editorial selection, not a leaderboard* ("순서와 순위는 무관하다"). *Why it fits:* this is exactly how IZM/Music Y look authoritative with zero personal-record machinery, and it's pure curation of the owner's own bucket — no crowd required. Source: https://www.izm.co.kr/posts?id=33746 ; http://musicy.kr/?c=best
- **Free-form review with optional half-star, calibrated to ONE voice (Letterboxd).** Letterboxd deliberately leaves the half-star meaning un-standardized so readers calibrate to a *specific person* — which is the natural strength of a single-author site (one consistent palate). *Why it fits:* turns the "no universal scale" weakness into the single-author selling point. Source: https://razzledazzlecr.com/blogs/articles/letterbox-ratings
- **Year-end "Best Albums of [year]" as a first-class, prominent rail (Bandcamp Daily, Pitchfork, IZM, AOTY all run it).** Universally the most legible authority artifact; cheap for one author to produce once content exists, and already a roadmap candidate. *Why it fits:* it's curation of the owner's own bucket, inert-but-graceful at near-zero content. Source: https://daily.bandcamp.com/best-of-2025/the-best-albums-of-2025-essential-releases
- **A discovery newsletter that is just curated editorial picks (Bandcamp "Notes" on Substack).** A once/twice-weekly "here's what I bucketed + wrote" email is reproducible by one author and needs no community. *Why it fits:* it's the owner broadcasting their own record, not aggregating others'. Source: https://bandcamp.substack.com/about
- **Score gated behind the article, not a homepage leaderboard (IZM).** IZM keeps the per-review score inside the article (only a "별점" *facet* for discovery), so the home reads as a publication, not a ranking board — preserving editorial tone over scoreboard tone. *Why it fits:* keeps myblog on the magazine side of the home without discarding the rating. Source: https://www.izm.co.kr/review

## What it must NOT borrow

- **Crowd-aggregation as the authority source (RYM Bayesian weighting; Letterboxd 25k-floor canon; AOTY User Score).** These manufacture "objective" rankings by averaging thousands of logs — a single author has no crowd, so any imitation is **fake scale / fake community data**, explicitly ruled out in SHARED CONTEXT. *Why it breaks:* one person can't be a credible "average"; the lesson is to supply authority through *voice*, not weighting. Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/wiki/RYM:Weighting%20FAQ ; https://letterboxd.com/official/list/letterboxds-top-500-films/
- **A dual Critic-Score + User-Score on every album (AOTY).** The User Score is the community-vote model already deliberately dropped; on a solo site it's either empty or fabricated. *Why it breaks:* it re-introduces the community layer myblog removed and would sit hollow at near-zero traffic. Source: https://www.albumoftheyear.org/
- **Reader/community scoring bolted under the verdict (Pitchfork 2026 Reader Score, RT-style).** Needs ≥5 raters per album to even display — guaranteed empty for a single-author site, and reintroduces the dropped community apparatus. *Why it breaks:* same fake-scale trap; the per-album aggregate never populates. Source: https://axios.com/2026/01/20/pitchfork-subscription-reader-scores-comments
- **A reviews paywall / "N free reviews per month" gate (Pitchfork).** With near-zero content and no audience, a paywall throttles the only thing the site needs (readership) and signals scale that doesn't exist. *Why it breaks:* premature monetization against an empty catalog. Source: https://exclaim.ca/music/article/pitchfork-paywalls-reviews-launches-subscription-service
- **A member/profile/collection system built for OTHER users (RYM friends/shoutbox/PMs; AOTY following; Letterboxd follow-graph).** myblog is single-author with community features deliberately dropped; per-*reader* profiles, friend graphs, and shoutboxes are dead weight. *Why it breaks:* the personal-record layer here is the OWNER's one bucket, not a multi-user social graph. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_Your_Music
- **Separating the magazine from the collection (Bandcamp's two-surface model).** Bandcamp keeps critical prose (Daily) and the personal collection (fan page) as disconnected properties bridged only by a buy link. myblog's entire bet is the *opposite* — fuse them so the owner's bucket IS the critical unit. *Why it breaks:* copying the split would discard myblog's one differentiator. Source: https://bandcamp.com/fans
- **Archaic plumbing as "editorial purity" (음악취향Y: HTTP-only, visitor counter, no RSS/subscribe/accounts).** Music Y's strong editorial voice coexists with broken modern plumbing — that's a cost, not a virtue. *Why it breaks:* myblog already has RSS and a modern stack; regressing to a hit-counter aesthetic loses syndication and trust for nothing. Source: http://musicy.kr/
- **Opaque/unlabeled iconography in the top nav (RYM `$`/`[X]` icons; Letterboxd's unlabeled "lightning-bolt").** Both were called out as UX failures. *Why it breaks:* a small-content site can least afford navigation friction; label everything. Source: https://medium.com/@justicedsn/rateyourmusic-case-study-an-examination-of-ryms-ux-flaws-dca7f724462b
